GTXR / Test and FEA correlation

Shear modulus testing & FEA correlation

Plate-twist testing to measure the in-plane shear modulus of carbon fiber and Rohacell sandwich panels and validate an ANSYS ACP model for fin flutter predictions.

Nathaniel Murcin / Test apparatus design; co-author and presenter, AIAA 2024-85690

Why measure it

Fin flutter predictions need the effective shear modulus of the fin laminate, and composite properties depend on how the part was made. The team measured G for 5 in × 5 in sandwich panels (Rohacell foam core, quasi-isotropic carbon fiber facesheets) and compared it with an ANSYS ACP sandwich model. Agreement within 5% would validate the model for future fin design.

Apparatus design

I designed the plate-twist test apparatus. Two 1 in aluminum plates keep the rig stiff, so the measured displacement comes from the panel rather than the fixture.

Steel ball bearings at opposite corners replace the usual conical contact points, which tend to puncture the panel. The lower fixture is clocked 90° from the upper one to create the twisting couple, and both mount to an Instron 5982 load frame.

Results

Force and displacement data were reduced in MATLAB to a shear modulus for each panel.

In-plane shear modulus, G
SourceG (GPa)
Foam 13.396
Foam 23.386
Foam 33.474
Mean of three panels3.419
ANSYS ACP model3.281

Within 5%

The mean measured shear modulus was within 5% of the FEA prediction (mean difference about 4.2%), meeting the acceptance criterion set before the test. The validated value feeds fin flutter predictions.
